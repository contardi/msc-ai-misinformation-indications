  Correlation near 1.0 would mean the models rank documents the same way, so combining
  them could only average out noise. Values well below 1.0 mean they are keying on
  genuinely different signal, which is the precondition for an ensemble to help.

  wrote /app/results/ensemble_pareto.csv

  w=0.0 is the currently served model: ROC-AUC 0.7995, ECE 0.0694, harms 11.1% / 12.8%
  w=0.50 maximises ranking: ROC-AUC 0.8305 (+0.0310), ECE 0.1445 (+0.0750)

  Weights that improve ranking AND leave BOTH user-facing harms no worse than w=0:
    w=0.05  ROC-AUC 0.8056 (+0.0061)  ECE 0.0796 (+0.0102)  harms 10.7% / 12.2%
    w=0.10  ROC-AUC 0.8113 (+0.0118)  ECE 0.0855 (+0.0160)  harms 10.4% / 11.6%
    w=0.15  ROC-AUC 0.8164 (+0.0169)  ECE 0.0936 (+0.0242)  harms 10.7% / 11.6%

  CAUTION: this is chosen over only three corpora, so treat the specific weight as
  indicative rather than tuned. Reporting the whole curve is the defensible move;
  quoting a single optimum selected on the same data that measured it is not.

  ECE rising monotonically with w means there is no free lunch on the honesty of the
  number, so the choice of w is an explicit trade rather than an optimisation. That is
  the same shape as the SVM (best F1, no predict_proba) and BERTugues (equal ranking,
  non-transferring probabilities): the best model by the headline metric is not the
  model that can ship.
